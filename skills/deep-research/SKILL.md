# Deep research

Start with the decision or question the reader needs answered. Identify the facts that could change the answer, the relevant dates and the evidence that would settle them. For “today” or “this week”, take the local date and offset from Anda's runtime context and establish the time window.

1. Look at the available callable names. Load a configured search or fetch tool through `tools_select`, or use `browser_tabs` and `browser_page` with a connected browser. If online access is unavailable, use supplied sources and identify the claims still needing verification.
2. Search from several relevant angles. Start with the specification, dataset, original study or first-party account. Follow citations to the underlying evidence. Check an independent source for consequential or disputed claims.
3. Open the sources you rely on. Record the URL, publication or revision date, and the passage, table or result that supports each significant claim. For recent events, distinguish the event date from the reporting date.
4. Reconcile apparent disagreements by comparing definitions, population, methods and dates. If the evidence still conflicts, show the disagreement and how it affects the answer. Stop when further searches no longer change the answer or a material gap is clear.

Deliver the requested answer, report or other artifact in the user's language. Link claims to the sources that support them. Distinguish reported results from your interpretation. If the research feeds another artifact, carry the citations into that artifact.
